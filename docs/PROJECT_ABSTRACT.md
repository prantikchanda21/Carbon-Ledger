# Carbon Ledger — Project Abstract

Carbon Ledger is a research-oriented, carbon-aware cloud operations and treasury intelligence platform that evaluates how computational workloads can be distributed across 20 geographic cloud regions. Cloud placement decisions involve competing objectives: electricity-related carbon intensity, infrastructure and energy cost, network latency, renewable-energy availability, policy compliance, and regional capacity. Carbon Ledger brings these trade-offs into an interactive decision environment, allowing users to examine scenario-dependent allocation strategies, operational footprints, cost estimates, and treasury outcomes.

The platform is built with Next.js, React, and TypeScript and supports scenario-responsive telemetry, weather-informed planning using Open-Meteo, workload scheduling experiments, allocation visualizations, research benchmarks, reporting tools, and optional AI-assisted explanations. Its financial layer models investment and hedging allocations alongside cloud expenditure, distinguishing gross investment APY from operating-cost-adjusted net yield. Regional carbon inputs are modeled while Open-Meteo can supply weather forecasts; the results are scenario estimates rather than audited real-world emissions or realized returns.

Quantum computing is investigated through multiple experimental optimization engines. Standard QAOA, fluid-inspired XY-QAOA, and gaseous-inspired variants generate simulated quantum proposals that can be combined with GLPK classical optimization. The separate A*-Guided Quantum Ensemble uses four parallel 7-qubit CPU statevector simulations at three rounds, combining Grover-style threshold amplification, hypercube quantum walks, digitized annealing-style evolution, and a classical A*-inspired candidate frontier. This independent ensemble supplies its own capacity-feasible regional allocation without an analytical or GLPK allocation fallback; carbon, cost, latency, risk, and net treasury outcomes are recalculated from those weights. The quantum ensemble remains *hybrid quantum-classical* because candidate encoding, objective calculations, frontier exploration, and validation are classical and the quantum circuits are simulated on a CPU.

In a previously recorded benchmark of 32 synthetic 20-region scenarios, the A*-Guided Ensemble reported a 1.62% mean objective gap to a SciPy HiGHS linear-programming optimum and a 1.87 ms warm median runtime. These results are not direct GLPK benchmark results and do not demonstrate quantum computational advantage. Carbon Ledger's core contribution is an interpretable, reproducible environment for exploring the operational and financial consequences of classical and quantum-informed scheduling choices rather than claiming a production-ready quantum speedup.
